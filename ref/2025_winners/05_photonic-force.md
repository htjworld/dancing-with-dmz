## Project summary

AakashNet is an indigenous Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite network designed to provide secure, high-speed, and affordable internet connectivity across India’s diverse terrain. We developed a phased-array based user terminal capable of dynamic beam steering through Ku/Ka band communication, linking directly with AakashNet satellites in orbit. These satellites form a laser-linked mesh network, enabling continuous data relay with minimal latency and reduced ground dependency.

The system directly addresses India’s connectivity challenges—bridging the digital divide in rural regions, providing encrypted communication for defense posts, and delivering in-flight and maritime internet without relying on foreign networks. With hardware locally produced by Indian agencies like ISRO, HAL, and BHEL, AakashNet ensures technological sovereignty and strategic independence.

By combining indigenous satellite manufacturing, reusable ISRO launches, and adaptive phased-array ground technology, AakashNet transforms connectivity into a national infrastructure asset—empowering governance, education, defense, and the digital economy.

## Project details

AakashNet is an indigenous Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite-based broadband communication system designed to deliver secure, high-speed, and reliable connectivity across India’s diverse landscapes — from rural villages to defense borders, ships, and aircraft. It solves India’s connectivity gap by replacing dependency on terrestrial infrastructure like fiber and towers with a network of locally manufactured satellites and smart phased-array user terminals.
How It Works:
Each user terminal — the Aakash Jodak dish — houses around 1280 phased-array antennas that electronically steer narrow beams toward rapidly orbiting AakashNet satellites via the Ku/Ka band. These satellites communicate not only with ground stations but also with each other through laser-based inter-satellite links, creating a resilient, low-latency communication mesh in space. Ground gateways connected to ISRO’s ISTRAC manage satellite telemetry, internet routing, and network optimization.
Benefits:
- Universal Access: Extends broadband to remote regions where fiber is impractical.
- National Security: Provides fully encrypted, sovereign communication lines for defense and governance.
- Economic Efficiency: Indigenous mass manufacturing and reusable launch systems drastically lower per-satellite and terminal costs.
- Operational Flexibility: Mobile connectivity for aviation, maritime, and defense mobility.
- Scalable Infrastructure: A modular system that can expand coverage and capacity as needed.
Intended Impact:
AakashNet bridges India’s digital divide by connecting 700+ million offline citizens, strengthening national defense autonomy, and empowering sectors like education, healthcare, and e-governance. It supports the Digital India and Atmanirbhar Bharat missions by ensuring that India’s communication backbone is built, managed, and secured within its borders.
Development Tools and Technology Stack:
- Frontend: HTML, CSS (Tailwind), JavaScript
- Hosting: Locally hosted interface (no backend), simulating user dashboard and satellite control visualization.
- Hardware Simulation: Modeled phased-array terminal design and link budgeting through MATLAB and OpenEMS (for RF performance).
- Software Logic: Prototype control and visualization scripts written in JavaScript for demonstrating network topology, beam switching, and data throughput visualization.
- Design Assets: 2D/3D mockups of terminal hardware and coverage dashboard for real-time user interaction.
Creativity and Innovation:
The project reimagines satellite internet as a sovereign national infrastructure rather than a private service. Its phased-array antenna approach eliminates mechanical tracking, enabling compact, low-maintenance terminals. The use of a laser-linked mesh among satellites reduces ground dependency and enhances latency performance. On the software side, the frontend dashboard was built using lightweight web technologies (HTML, Tailwind CSS, JavaScript) to visualize live satellite coverage, beam steering, and bandwidth analytics — without requiring a backend, making it simple and portable.
Design Considerations:
- Affordability: Designed for mass production with hardware cost capped at ₹23,000 per terminal.
- Sovereignty: Full Indian control over design, manufacturing, and launch (via ISRO–HAL–BHEL collaboration).
- Reliability: Resilient network with multiple failover paths through inter-satellite communication.
- Security: AES-256 and WPA3 standards with a roadmap toward Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC).
- Scalability: Architecture supports commercial, rural, and defense-grade variants with tailored service plans.
In essence, AakashNet is more than just a satellite internet solution, it’s a step toward India’s digital self-reliance, enabling universal, secure, and sovereign connectivity across every corner of the nation.
